# Understanding city image communication through agenda-setting theory and an agent-based approach: A case study of Macau
In the context of globalization, city image has become an important component of urban competitiveness. It not only reflects a city’s economic development, cultural characteristics, and urban environment, but also plays a significant role in attracting capital, talent, and tourists. As an important means of shaping a city’s image, urban communication has attracted growing attention in communication studies. However, it remains relatively underexplored in urban studies, especially in systematic analyses grounded in agenda-setting theory. To address this gap, this study takes Macau as a case and develops an analytical framework for city communication and city image construction based on agenda-setting theory and an agent-based approach. Specifically, news articles from the WeChat official account Macau SAR Release are used as samples of local official media, while Macau-related news published by ‘Release’ accounts from other cities are treated as samples of non-local official media. Methodologically, this study develops a **multi-label news classification model**, constructs a **news topic co-occurrence network** and a **news topic similarity model**, and analyzes the construction of Macau’s city image from the three levels of agenda setting. The findings show that local and non-local official media share certain similarities but also exhibit clear differences in the salience of Macau-related issues, attribute framing, and issue associations, jointly constructing a multifaceted image of Macau encompassing cultural, economic, and political dimensions. This study provides a new analytical approach to city communication, offering a fresh perspective on city image research.
